Printing Methods

Six Printing Methods, Matched to What You're Actually Making

Not every print method belongs on every fabric. A soft cotton tee, a laminated waterproof shell, and a heavyweight fleece hoodie each need a different ink system, cure time and pressure setting to hold up long-term. Rather than defaulting to one process for every order, we match the method to your fabric, your durability requirement and your budget — and tell you honestly when a technique isn't the right fit for what you're building.

Plastisol Screen Printing

Our standard, most-requested print method. Plastisol sits on top of the fabric with strong opacity, which means bold colors and clean logos show up clearly even on black or navy garments. It's durable, consistent across large runs, and the default choice for corporate branding, workwear and everyday apparel.

Get a Quote

Water-Based Screen Printing

Water-based ink soaks into the fibers instead of sitting on top, so the final print has almost no texture — you feel the fabric, not the ink. This is the method we recommend for performance tees and lightweight technical layers, where a soft hand-feel matters more than maximum opacity.

Get a Quote

Puff Print

Foaming agents in the ink lift the print off the surface as it cures, creating a raised, tactile texture that catches the eye. Works best on heavier fabrics like hoodies, fleece and outerwear, where a flat print would get lost.

Get a Quote

Discharge Print

Instead of adding a layer of ink, discharge printing removes the garment's original dye and replaces it with color — the result is a soft, slightly faded finish with zero texture, closer to how a vintage tee looks after years of washing. Works only on cotton and cotton-heavy blends.

Get a Quote

Reflective & Safety Print

Micro-glass beads embedded directly into the ink reflect light back at its source, making this print method genuinely functional, not just decorative. Built for tactical gear, workwear and low-visibility outdoor apparel where safety compliance actually matters — tested for durability through repeated washing.

Get a Quote

Foil Print

A metallic foil layer bonded to the garment under heat and pressure, used for sharp, reflective branding accents on premium or limited-run collections. A finishing touch, not a full-coverage print method.
