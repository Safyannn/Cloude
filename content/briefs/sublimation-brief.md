## SECTION 7 — TEXT BLOCK 1 (SEO) — How Sublimation Works

**H2:** How Dye Sublimation Actually Gets a Design Into the Fabric

**Paragraph 1:** Sublimation ink is applied to transfer paper, then bonded to the garment using a heat press set to a precise time, temperature and pressure combination. Under heat, the ink converts directly from solid to gas and embeds itself into the fibers of the fabric — it doesn't sit on the surface the way a screen print or heat transfer does.

**Paragraph 2:** This process only works reliably on 100% polyester fabric or polyester-coated surfaces — it's why sublimation is the standard choice for performance sportswear and moisture-wicking technical apparel, which are typically polyester-based already.

---

## SECTION 8 — TEXT BLOCK 2 (SEO) — Why It Holds Up Long-Term

**H2:** Why Sublimated Graphics Outlast Standard Printed Designs

**Paragraph 1:** Because the ink bonds into the fabric itself instead of forming a layer on top, sublimated designs don't crack, peel or fade the way a surface-level print can after repeated washing. The print becomes part of the fabric's structure, which is why it holds detail and color for the life of the garment.

**Paragraph 2:** Sublimation also produces zero water waste and minimal material waste compared to some traditional printing methods, which matters increasingly to brands under pressure to reduce their environmental footprint without compromising print quality.

## SUGGESTED FAQ (ADD 6–8 FOR SEO/AI-SEARCH VALUE — SEE MASTER PROMPT'S GEO SECTION)

1. **What fabrics work with sublimation printing?**
Sublimation requires 100% polyester or polyester-coated fabric. It does not work reliably on cotton or cotton blends — for those, screen printing or heat transfer is a better fit.

2. **What's the difference between cut-and-sew sublimation and blank garment sublimation?**
Cut-and-sew sublimation prints on fabric panels before assembly, giving a cleaner result across seams. Blank garment sublimation prints on a finished garment, which is faster and more cost-effective but limited by existing seam placement.

3. **Does sublimation printing work on dark-colored garments?**
No — sublimation requires a light or white base fabric, since the ink is transparent and relies on the fabric's base color showing through.

4. **How durable is a sublimated print compared to screen printing?**
Because the ink bonds into the fabric itself rather than sitting on the surface, sublimated prints generally resist cracking, peeling and fading better than surface-level prints over repeated washing.

5. **Can you produce a fully manufactured, sublimated garment, not just print a blank?**
Yes — sublimation printing is one part of our full manufacturing capability, which includes fabric sourcing, cutting, sewing and finishing under the same production timeline.

6. **What's the minimum order quantity for sublimation printing?**
MOQs depend on the garment type and whether it's cut-and-sew or blank garment sublimation. Share your target product and quantity and we'll confirm exact minimums.
