Overview

Precision-Manufactured Cold Weather Gloves

Cold degrades grip, dexterity and safety, and it does it fast. Heliotrope Safety's cold-weather range is engineered to the three factors of EN 511 — convective cold, contact cold and water penetration — so buyers can specify genuine thermal performance rather than guessing from a lining photo. We build insulated leather, waterproof synthetics and thermal-lined coated knits for every winter environment.

Insulation is matched to the task: breathable Thinsulate™-class linings for active outdoor work, dense foam and fleece for static cold-store handling, and waterproof-breathable membranes for wet-cold conditions. Extended cuffs, wind-blocking shells and cold-flexible grip coatings keep crews working safely through the coldest shifts.

FAQ

Cold Weather Gloves — Frequently Asked

Technical and commercial answers for buyers specifying cold weather gloves at volume.

EN 511 rates three cold factors: convective cold (insulation against cold air, 0–4), contact cold (insulation against cold surfaces, 0–4), and water penetration (0 = leaks, 1 = waterproof). A glove marked 4-4-1 offers maximum insulation and is waterproof — the specification to look for in freezer and wet-cold work.
Static sub-zero handling calls for maximum insulation with less emphasis on breathability, so we recommend heavily foam- and fleece-lined gloves with extended cuffs and, ideally, a waterproof membrane. For active outdoor work, a breathable Thinsulate™-class lining prevents sweat build-up that would otherwise chill the hand.
Standard coatings can crack and harden in the cold. We use cold-flexible latex and nitrile formulations that stay pliable and grippy at sub-zero temperatures, so hands keep both warmth and secure grip in freezer and winter conditions.
Cold-weather demand is sharply seasonal. We advise confirming programs 8–10 weeks before your peak season so production and shipping complete before the cold arrives. We can hold approved stock and release against call-off to smooth this.
